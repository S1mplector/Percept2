# Community Code of Conduct

## Our commitment

Java Vector Nexus (JVN) is built by people with different backgrounds,
experience levels, identities, and communication styles. We are committed to a
community where people can contribute, ask questions, review work, and disagree
about technical decisions without harassment or intimidation.

Participation is a privilege, not a right. Everyone participating in JVN spaces
must follow this policy, including maintainers.

## Scope

This policy applies to:

- the JVN repositories, issues, pull requests, reviews, and discussions;
- project-managed chat, events, documentation, and other community spaces;
- private communication about project work;
- public conduct when someone is representing JVN or using an official project
  role.

It also applies to screenshots, recordings, generated content, links, and other
material posted in those spaces.

## Expected behavior

JVN participants are expected to:

- discuss the work rather than attacking the person;
- give specific, actionable feedback and explain blocking concerns;
- support technical claims with code, documentation, tests, or reproducible
  evidence when practical;
- make room for questions and avoid treating unfamiliarity as incompetence;
- accept correction, acknowledge mistakes, and repair harm where possible;
- respect names, pronouns, privacy, personal boundaries, and accessibility
  needs;
- credit prior work and disclose relevant conflicts of interest;
- keep reviews and discussions focused, and move extended disagreements to a
  better venue when asked;
- avoid sharing secrets, private project content, or personal information in
  logs and reproductions.

Good-faith disagreement is welcome. Maintainers may reject an idea or
contribution for scope, compatibility, maintenance, or technical reasons
without that decision being a conduct violation. The manner in which a decision
is communicated remains covered by this policy.

## Unacceptable behavior

The following behavior is not accepted:

- harassment, threats, stalking, or deliberate intimidation;
- discrimination or derogatory comments based on identity, background,
  disability, appearance, or level of experience;
- sexualized language, imagery, attention, or advances;
- insults, personal attacks, humiliation, or sustained hostile communication;
- doxxing or publishing private information without clear permission;
- impersonation, deceptive attribution, or knowingly false claims about another
  participant;
- deliberate disruption, spam, repeated off-topic contact, or evasion of
  moderation controls;
- pressuring someone to disclose private, medical, employment, or identity
  information;
- retaliation against a reporter, witness, moderator, or person who declines an
  interaction;
- knowingly weaponizing conduct, security, or review processes to harass or
  silence others.

## Reporting a concern

Do not post sensitive conduct reports in a public issue.

Contact the repository owner, [@S1mplector](https://github.com/S1mplector),
through a private contact method published on that GitHub profile. Include only
what is needed to investigate:

- links or message identifiers;
- dates and approximate times;
- a factual description of what occurred;
- relevant context and prior requests to stop;
- whether there is an immediate safety or privacy concern;
- the outcome you are requesting, if any.

If no private contact method is available, open a minimal issue asking for a
private conduct contact. Do not include names, screenshots, or incident details
in that issue. For violations of GitHub's platform policies, reporters may also
use GitHub's built-in report or block controls.

Use [SECURITY.md](SECURITY.md) for vulnerabilities. A security advisory is not a
general-purpose conduct channel unless the report also contains a security
issue.

## Handling reports

Maintainers handling a report will:

1. prioritize immediate safety and prevent further exposure of private
   information;
2. acknowledge the report when a safe reply channel is available;
3. review available evidence and seek only the clarification needed;
4. give an affected participant a reasonable opportunity to respond when doing
   so is safe and appropriate;
5. choose a proportionate response and document the reasoning privately;
6. share the outcome with the reporter when privacy and safety permit.

Report details will be limited to people needed to handle the matter. Complete
confidentiality cannot be guaranteed when investigation or enforcement
requires disclosure, but unnecessary identifying details will not be shared.

A maintainer with a personal conflict, or who is the subject of a report, should
not make the enforcement decision when another appropriate maintainer is
available. JVN may not always have an independent maintainer available; this
limitation will be stated honestly rather than implying an independent appeals
process exists.

## Enforcement

Maintainers may remove or edit content, close or lock discussions, reject
contributions, limit contact, or temporarily or permanently restrict
participation. Responses depend on severity, impact, context, history, and
willingness to correct the behavior.

Typical responses are:

1. **Correction** — a private or public request to stop or edit specific
   behavior.
2. **Warning** — a documented warning describing the violation and expected
   change.
3. **Temporary restriction** — a time-limited pause from interaction,
   contribution, or project spaces.
4. **Permanent restriction** — removal from project spaces or roles for severe
   behavior, retaliation, evasion, or repeated violations.

Severe threats, doxxing, targeted harassment, or credible safety risks may move
directly to a permanent restriction. Enforcement is based on behavior and
impact, not a contributor's status, tenure, or technical value.

## Appeals and corrections

A person subject to enforcement may request one reconsideration by replying
privately with material context, a factual correction, or evidence that the
response was disproportionate. Repeating the original argument without new
information, contacting uninvolved contributors, or retaliating against a
reporter is not an appeal.

Maintainers may correct or clarify an enforcement record when new evidence
shows that a material mistake was made.
